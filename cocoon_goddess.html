The Outcasts

Outcasts: Rubik the Burgundy, Cassiopeia, and Xavier mill around here awkwardly playing cards and sharing stories. They are extremely bored and welcome any distraction.

North passage: originally the entrance to the temple. Completely impassable. Even if the rubble were cleared the administrative building has been constructed on top.

Dry Pool

Pool: this pool once allowed visitors to wash themselves upon entry. It now contains nothing but dust and grime.

Drain: a rusted iron grill in the centre of the pool covers a narrow pipe leading to [ROOM].

Doves: 1d12 Unborn Doves peck idly at the cracks in the pool. They are incredibly tame.

Initiates' Passage

Passage: this once provided easy access between the temple entrance and the initiates' quarters.

Secret doors: open normally from this side.

Scarred Door

Southeast doorway: covered in an enormous membrane of scarred, scabbed, blistered flesh. Attempts to physically damage it only cause it to scab over even harder. Healing spells or potions cause it to recede for six turns.

Western passage: rubble is loosely packed and could be safely cleared in 12 person-hours.

Chamber of Communion

Chair of communion: a plush reclining chair stands in the centre of the room facing west. A lever lies to its north, with a red handprint around the handle, and a strange helmet bristling with needles hangs by a cord from the ceiling.

Pulling the lever: the needles contract into the helmet wearer's skull with a loud thunk. Surprisingly, this causes no harm, and instead allows the wearer to commune directly with the Hand Incarnadine [LINK], who is currently half-digested by Thorns-by-Night [LINK] and comprises half of the Cocoon Goddess [LINK]. He will beg the wearer for help and/or death.

Handprints: red handprints are painted in a band around the room at chest height. Most do nothing when touched. One in the centre of the north wall causes an electrical shock (4d4 damage, save vs spells for half); one in the centre of the east wall causes permanent paralysis (save vs spells to resist, can be cured by Cure Light Wounds or Dispel Magic; one in the centre of the south wall grants access to the treasury.

Treasury of the Hand Incarnadine

Treasure: 12,000gp in coins, 8,000gp in gems and jewellery, a Hammer of Mobility[TODO], a Potion of Greater Healing, and a scroll of Raise Dead.

Patriarch's Quarters

Bed: ornate four-poster bed against western wall with soiled silk sheets, bedframe worth 250gp if disassembled.

Desk: wooden desk against south wall piled with papers. Zombified Patriarch lies slumped over desk, waiting to attack if intruders come close.

Papers: Old Imperial. Mostly mundane accounting and bureaucratic paperwork, but one drawer contains a missive from the imperial spymaster warning of an impending attack by an avatar of Thorns-by-Night [LINK] and instructing use of the Anathema Device [LINK].

Sutured Door

East doorway: sutured closed by Steel Suture.

Potion Storage

Corpses: two Zombified Initiates lie slumped against eastern wall. One has a single casting of Steel Suture prepared. Both animate and attack if anyone enters the room.

Potions: potion rack on south wall contains Potion of Lesser Healing, Potion of Antivenom, Potion of Truesight, two potion bottles containing strong painkillers (100gp each), and one potion bottle containing red dye.

Supplies: shelves on east wall contain miscellaneous medical supplies (bandages, poultices, medicinal herbs, etc.), all rotted beyond use.

South Ward

Empty: originally a patient ward, but all furniture has been sold or burned for firewood. Only dust remains.

West Ward

Empty: originally a patient ward, but all furniture has been sold or burned for firewood. Only dust remains.

Chapel of the Hand Incarnadine

Cremation: deceased members of the Second Society [LINK] are cremated here, as shown by a large scorch mark in the centre of the room and around fifty names scratched into the western wall.

Stone altar: at eastern end of room, bare. It has a locked secret compartment, trapped with a hollow needle that implants a Worm of Despair (save vs death to resist). Inside are 2200gp in coins, a Continually Lit rose gold candelabra worth 900gp, a bottle of fine wine worth 500gp, and three flasks of Holy Oil.

Initiate Boundary

Trapdoor: in centre of room, outlined and marked with an X in white chalk. Opens to a shaft 40ft down to [ROOM].

West door: false door with no doorhandle, painted with a blue handprint. Touching it causes an electric shock (1d4 damage, save vs spells for half).

North door: false door with no doorhandle, painted with a black handprint. Touching it causes paralysis for 12 turns (save vs spells to resist).

East door: red hand door [LINK].

Initiate Corridor

West door: opens normally from this side.

Doves: 3-in-6 chance of 1d6 Unborn Doves perched on ballustrade overlooking the well.

Library of the Hand Incarnadine

Books: mostly already looted, or too decayed to read. Copies of Vernon's Body and Floral Remedies remain readable.

Scroll rack: prominent red scroll with gold trim, if read save vs spells or be cursed such that anything spoken, written, or signed comes out as "I stole from the sacred clerical library of the Isabellan Temple of the Hand Incarnadine". Rack also contains two scrolls of Cure Light Wounds, a scroll of Cure Disease, and a scroll of Steel Suture.

Initiates' Quarters

Furniture: six beds, decayed and worthless, each with matching wooden lockboxes. Searching thoroughly yields 60gp in coins and personal effects worth 35gp.

Disgusting corpse: partially-rotten Zombified Initiate, almost melted into bed, attacks if disturbed. Being struck causes Meatrot (save vs death to resist).

Day Room

Furniture: 13 non-matching wooden chairs, two sofas, a coffee table, Snowdrop's desk covered in translation papers, firepit with cooking equipment, barrels of food, amphorae of water.

People: during daytime, 5d6 members of Second Society [LINK] chatting, knitting, cooking, eating, etc.

Dormitory

Beds: 21 tightly packed non-matching beds, stuffed with lice-ridden straw. During night, 5d6 members of Second Society here resting as best they can.

Victory's Office

Furniture: two desks and five cabinets completely stuffed with papers. Braille typewriter on one desk. Simple bed in one corner. Locked chest contains 800gp in coins, the total liquid wealth of the Second Society [LINK].

Papers: Whisperbraille. Approximately 30% are accounting/bureaucratic paperwork, 65% are correspondences with a network of miscellaneous suppliers, merchants, and informants throughout Isabella, and the remaining 5% are detailed records of the intimate secrets of every member of the Second Society (including the party) alongside prayers dedicated to The Unutterable [LINK].

Secret door: opened by depressing hidden button on ceiling, perfectly sized for Victory's cane. Leads to [ROOM].

Saxifrage's Workshop

Pottery: potter's wheel, clay, plates and vases, paints and dyes. 1-in-6 chance Victory is here working on pottery.

Storage: crates, barrels, scrap metal, scrap wood, scrap cloth, pile of coal, spare mobility aids, torches, candles.

South of the Well

Platform: wooden platform attached by ropes to crane on the surface, raised at dawn and lowered at dusk. Used to transport new members of the Second Society [LINK], supplies, and mail.

Crypt entrance: spiked shut unless someone is there. Plaque above door reads "Those we could not save" in Old Imperial, with Common Imperial translation scrawled in white chalk next to it.

West of the Well

Portcullis: 20ft square bronze portcullis, old but in good condition.

North stairs: plaque above reads "Only initiates may pass this point" in Old Imperial, with Common Imperial translation scrawled in white chalk next to it.

South door: locked and marked with stylised key in white chalk.

A Useless Lever

Lever: does nothing when pulled. Chains connecting portcullis (West of the Well) to counterweight (Flooded Workshop) are broken.

South: ledge overlooks Flooded Workshop.

Flooded Workshop

Counterweight: large stone square 20ft × 20ft × 1/2ft leaning against west wall. Was connected by chains into the ceiling and across to portcullis (West of the Well) but the chains have broken with age.

Shelving: glass eyes and well constructed but nonfunctional prosthetics worth 500gp total; crutches, splints, syringes, sutures, etc. ruined by water damage.

Workbench: tools worth 100gp, waterlogged papers, and an Unfinished Baton of Speech.

Hall of the Weeping Violinist

Statue: a statue of a weeping woman with widow's bellflowers woven into her hair stands atop a 10ft wide 1ft high dais to the south. This is a teleportation statue [DETAILS GO HERE].

Portcullis: a portcullis stands at the east entrance. It is operated by a lever on the northeast wall. Normally the portcullis is left down to prevent wandering creatures entering the Second Society.

Exits: the southeast door has an inverted U drawn on it in chalk. The northwest corridor has an inverted U drawn on the ground at the entrance, and smells strongly of smoke.

Chamber of Smokey Apparitions

Chimney: smoke pours from a 3ft square hole in the centre of the chamber floor, leading directly to the Crematorium below.

Smoke: the entire room is filled with thick choking smoke, causing 1hp damage per round to those inhaling it. Covering one's mouth and nose reduces this to 1hp every other round, while those with lung damage take double damage. Wailing faces can occasionally be glimpsed in the shifting whorls of smoke.

Summoning apparitions: burning a corpse in the Crematorium summons an apparition of the creature in the smoke here lasting for one turn. The apparition cannot move or attack, but can be communicated with as though it were alive.

Crypts of Clemency

Coffins: simple stone coffins line both the northwest and southeast walls. All of them lie open and already looted, containing nothing but shriveled corpses wrapped in funerary shrouds. The lids bear names, dates (all more than 800 years ago), and causes of death, usually disease or injury; these were patients of the Well of Clemency who died in the course of treatment.

Ventilation shaft: to the west, at floor height, a horizontal stone shaft 1.5ft tall, 2ft wide extends into the rock. It lies covered by one of the coffins and not immediately obvious from the entrance.

East Atrium

Balconies: 20ft up, there are balconies with 2ft balustrades overlooking the lower floor on the north, east, and west sides.

Triptych mural: three old faded murals cover the southwest, west, and northwest walls at the ground floor.

Vernon's Repose

Urn: a 5ft tall ornate terracotta urn decorated with geometric patterns in lapis lazuli, worth around 2500gp, stands on a pedestal to the northeast. It contains the ashes of Patriarch Vernon, former high priest of the Hand Incarnadine. Shortly before death his consciousness was transferred into the urn by means of a Magic Jar spell, in case he is needed again. He remains there in a state of dormancy until the urn is touched, at which point he will attempt to possess whoever is nearest.

Patriarch Vernon: he is a 9th level cleric with the following spells prepared: Cure Light Wounds × 2, Remove Fear; Steel Suture × 2, Hold Person; Cure Disease × 2, Umbilical Cord; Cure Serious Wounds, Neutralise Poison; Raise Dead × 2. He is disgusted by possessing a disabled person and demands an abled body to possess instead. He is also horrified to learn The Hand Incarnadine is dead—note this means he is unable to prepare more spells once his current ones are cast. Nonetheless he will abide by his vows and heal the sick to the best of his ability.

Door: opens normally from this side (with doorhandle).

Looted Tomb

Hall of Signs

Hungry Graveyard

Graveyard: the ground here is soft clotting grave dirt, dotted with crooked tombstones with faded inscriptions.

Sinking: the grave dirt sucks anything heavy down like quicksand, where it sinks through the ground into the Council of Vermin. Victims grasping at the tombstones will only pull the tombstone through with them. This unenviable fate can be avoided by use of levitation, sprinting through the room, jumping from tombstone to tombstone, wearing snowshoes, etc.

An Inauspicious Sight

Bloodstains: splatters of dried blood coat the centre of the room, with a large smear suggesting a body dragged to the northwest.

Ghouls: three ghouls arrive from the Demesne of the Ghoul Princess in 1d3 rounds to greet guests. They will attempt to circle around to prevent retreat and corral guests towards the princess.

Burial niches line all the walls from floor to ceiling. Searching them thoroughly takes six turns and yields 12gp in coins. One niche contains a partially animated skeletal hand that grasps at anything that moves (startling, but harmless).

Demesne of the Ghoul Princess

Ghoul princess: a Ghoul wearing a rose gold tiara worth 1200gp, tattered remains of silk robes, and wielding a femur as a sceptre. The only thing she loves more than entertaining guests is feasting upon human flesh. She speaks High Imperial only.

Ghoul courtiers: three Ghouls are here prostrating themselves before the princess, while a fourth is on all fours being used as her throne. They unquestioningly obey anyone wearing the princess's tiara. They understand High Imperial but do not speak.

Gore: shreds of dessicated flesh and dried blood coat the floor where previous guests have been cannibalised. Piles of shattered bones lie swept into the corners.

Treasure: around the princess's feet are piled 780gp in coins, 300gp in gems, a set of studded leather armour +2, a glaive, two throwing axes +1, a Crest of the Falcon, and a scroll case containing scrolls of Dimension Door, Growth of Plants, and Glyph (Petrification).

Burial niches line all the walls from floor to ceiling. Searching them thoroughly takes six turns and yields 6gp in coins and two swords rusted beyond use. One niche contains a crude shrine to the Yearning Maw set up by the princess.

Banquet of Doom

Banquet table: a simple wooden table holds a sumptuous banquet, including bowls of fruit, stuffed quail, a leg of lamb, a whole roasted suckling pig, etc. In reality this is simply an illusion cast over a pile of rotting maggot-ridden meat. Food taken from the table retains its illusion for six turns. Missing food is magically replaced with more on the table when unobserved.

Eating from the banquet: it is delicious and causes no immediate harm. After six turns the eater must save vs spells. On a success they get away with just being incapacitated by gastrointestinal illness for 2d12 days. On a failure, their skin turns pallid, their eyes go dim, and their phalanges burst through their fingertips as raking claws, as they are transformed into a Ghoul!

Ghouls: the ghouls from Demesne of the Ghoul Princess are aware of the secret door and will come in 1d3 rounds if they hear significant noise. They do not eat from the banquet; due to its enchanted nature it provides no sustenance to them.

Burial niches line all the walls from floor to ceiling. Searching them thoroughly takes six turns and yields 10gp in coins and a silver ring worth 25gp.

Five Boxed Wizards

Coffins: five simple stone coffins line the southwest wall. Each is engraved with a name and contains a Skeleton with one first level spell prepared, wielding a gemstone-tipped staff worth 50gp. If any coffin is opened then all skeletons simultaneously animate and emerge from their coffins to attack. From northwest to southeast, the coffins contain:

  • Amity: casts Charm Person, wields a jade-tipped staff.
  • Clarity: casts Light, wields a citrine-tipped staff.
  • Dignity: casts Spook, wields a moonstone-tipped staff. This coffin additionally contains a Wand of Portalcraft, concealed by cobwebs and dust.
  • Verity: casts Chromatic Orb (Light), wields a clear quartz-tipped staff.
  • Serenity: casts Shield, wields a lapis lazuli-tipped staff.

Mural: a large mural on the southwest wall depicts six wizards joining hands in a circle and dancing in clearing ringed by standing stones [LINK]. The artistry is enthusiastic but not especially good.

Burying Sincerity: if Sincerity's body is brought from [ROOM] and entombed similarly to her former companions, the party is granted 1000xp and the ability to see through all her illusions, plus a 1-in-6 chance to see through any illusion in general.

A Convenient Lever

Lever: by default both lever and portcullis are down. Approaching from the west, one could conveniently reach one's arm through to raise the lever, however this causes the portcullis to slam up into the ceiling, dragging the unfortunate arm with it and causing 1d12 damage (no save). The shoulder joint is shattered and the arm cannot be used without first casting Cure Serious Wounds.

The Most Beautiful Woman Who Ever Lived

Coffin: a finely carved marble coffin lies on a plinth in the centre of the room. The inscription simply reads "The Most Beautiful Woman Who Ever Lived".

Statue: incredibly impressive solid lapis lazuli statue is actually a Lapis Living Statue that animates and attacks if the coffin is tampered with. It does not pursue out of sight of the coffin.

Inside the coffin: an exquisitely beautiful corpse rests on a bed of widow's bellflowers, both magically preserved. The flowers immediately release a cloud of pollen causing all within 5ft to save vs death or fall asleep. The corpse wears a Ring of Glamour and Heels of Fascination. She is so heartbreakingly beautiful that anybody seeing her has a 1-in-6 chance of falling in love on the spot. If kept well preserved, the corpse could potentially be sold for 5000gp to somebody with sufficiently strange tastes.

Sincerity's Secret Study

Study: this room has been converted into a magical research laboratory. Several mismatched wooden desks are laden with glass prisms, small gemstones, pigments, tuning forks, and Esoteric tomes regarding optics. Collectively these are worth 500gp, but can count as 4000gp towards researching Illusionist spells or magic items.

Sincerity: the dessicated corpse of the Illusionist Sincerity lies slumped over the desk. When approached, a Magic Mouth declares "I protect these crypts still! Leave me and my domain in peace or be cursed forevermore!" This is a bluff, the corpse is inanimate and harmless. Her hand still loosely grasps an empty paper packet with poison residue. At her feet lies an opal-tipped staff worth 50gp.

Lever: operates the portcullis at A Sharp Squeeze. It is currently down.

Burial niches line all the walls from floor to ceiling. Searching them thoroughly takes six turns and yields 34gp in coins, a decaying cloth pouch containing five rubies worth 100gp each, and Sincerity's Spellbook.

A Sharp Squeeze

Portcullis: this portcullis is down, unless raised using the lever at Sincerity's Secret Study. The bottom of one of the vertical bars is broken off, leaving a narrow gap that someone without armour might be able to squeeze through, but the twisted metal edges would cause 1d4 damage in the process. Breaking or sanding off the sharp edge would take six turns of noisy work.

A Peaceful Corner

Bench: a simple stone bench lies against the east wall.

Burial niches line all the walls from floor to ceiling. Searching them thoroughly takes six turns and yields 8gp in coins.

Shadowy Reflections

Reflections: entering the corridor causes identical shadowy reflections of the party to appear from the other end, as though there were a mirror halfway through the corridor. The reflections perfectly mirror every action. They are corporeal while visible; attacking causes one's weapons to harmlessly bounce off the reflections' weapons.

Passage: the reflections are incorporeal if not visible to anyone; simply closing one's eyes and walking forwards allows passage.

Tomb of the Twins

Furniture: the room contains well made but delicate furniture: a chaise longue (500gp), two easy chairs (125gp each), and a coffee table (250gp) on which sits a fine china vase (1000gp) containing two sheafs of feathergrass. The furniture is almost certain to be damaged if combat breaks out.

Coffin: an unusually wide coffin sits at the north of the room. Within are two old corpses locked in a tight embrace (The Twins). They each wear Robes of the Dancer, Amulets of Reunion, and amethyst studded belts worth 600gp each. None of these can be obtained without pulling the corpses apart, which causes them to animate and attack.

Hidden compartment: an illusory wall conceals a 4ft high nook, within which is a locked chest containing 1200gp in coins, 2400gp in gems and jewellery, hundreds of letters of appreciation to the twin dancers, and two crystal champagne flutes worth 100gp each.

Curious Crossroads

West door: locked, and marked with a stylised key drawn in chalk.

Portcullis: down. The lever to raise it is visible 30ft away up the stairs on the other side.

Unconsciousness trap: an X is drawn in chalk on the floor, alongside an arrow pointing north. The 10ft section of corridor immediately ahead is enchanted such that anyone entering, even partially, immediately falls unconscious until they leave the area.

Secretive Grave

Coffin: a simple wooden coffin lies in the centre of the room. Inside is the mummified corpse of a woman with her finger raised to her lips. A wise observer might realise the finger is pointing the way to the secret door on the east wall.

Flowerless Graves

Graves: three graves are dug into the west, south, and east ends of the room, each headed by a faded illegible tombstone.

West grave: filled with gravel and has an ancient withered devil's powderbrush (representing earth) in a small clay pot.

South grave: filled with stagnant water and has an empty small clay pot. Putting a winter lily (representing water) here grants 2000xp, as the spirit of the deceased is able to move on.

East grave: filled with damp mud and has an empty small clay pot. Putting a winter lily (representing water), a daisy-of-the-meadow (representing combination), and a devil's powderbrush (representing earth) here grants 5000xp, as the spirit of the deceased is able to move on.

Burial niches line all the walls from floor to ceiling. Searching them thoroughly takes six turns and yields 22gp in coins.

The Council of Vermin

Cavern: this area has been dug out by the work of millions of tiny crawling insects and creatures over many years. Every wall and floor is latticed with small cracks and crevices, all alive with movement.

Miasma: the air reeks of death and rot. If spending at least a turn here, save vs death or contract Meatrot. Covering one's mouth and nose grants +3 to the save.

Pile of corpses: a 10ft high pile of perhaps fifty decomposing human corpses lies in the centre of the room, directly below the Hungry Graveyard. The pile crawls with feasting vermin. From it grow three meatblooms, which regrow at a rate of one per month. Searching the pile thoroughly takes 24 turns and yields a Sword of Seeking, a roundshield with a flower design, a hammer, five iron spikes, a brass candlestick worth 100gp, 31gp in coins, and the Sixfolk Reliquary which has been intentionally concealed here.

Vermin: there are 11HD of small insects (as several Insect Swarms, 24 Normal Rats, four Giant Rats, and seven Giant Centipedes. There is also a 1-in-6 chance Sir Sticky is visiting. The vermin usually avoid combat with humans, with the exception of those falling through from above, whom they swarm and eat alive while they are still disoriented and confused.

West Atrium

Fountain: a fountain is set into the east wall, comprising a statue of a woman (Galene) pouring water from an amphora into a broad basin. The water is clean and delicious thanks to a permanent Purify Food and Water spell.

Galene: the statue is sentient. She can speak and can taste any substance poured into the fountain, but cannot move or do anything else. Being deeply rooted into the surrounding stonework, she can answer any question about anything relating to stone anywhere in the dungeon, which she will be willing to do in return for tasting new substances:

  • Milk: "I've heard mother animals produce a special liquid for their children. I wonder what this mothering-liquid is like? I imagine it's very sweet.")
  • Tea: "People seem to regard this liquid highly, but isn't it just leaf-residue? It sounds quite revolting, frankly."
  • Fine wine (worth at least 500gp): "A passer-by once allowed me a draught of wine (I didn't care for it). He said apparently some will spend small fortunes on wine of exceptional quality. What sort of wine could possibly be that impressive?"
  • Ice: "Supposedly if water becomes cold enough it transforms into a type of white stone. Is this true?"
  • Snow: "I even heard someone say ice can fall from the sky in the form of little feathers. How can water be like stone and like a feather? This is nonsense, I'm quite sure."

Ventilation shaft: a horizontal stone shaft 1.5ft tall, 2ft wide emerges from the northeast wall 20ft above the ground.

Grate: a 10ft square bronze grate overlooks the room from 20ft up the west wall.

Crematorium

Cremation furnace: arched hole in north wall leads to compartment filled with magical flames, 3d6 damage per round. Chimney above leads to Chamber of Smokey Apparitions.

Cursed ash: waist-high drifts of ash piled against east and west walls, blocking east door from opening. The ash causes choking if inhaled (save vs breath weapons or die). Disturbing a small amount is unpleasant but nonfatal.

Bench: simple stone bench buried under ash pile on west wall. Underneath is a pewter rod (club +1, worth 250gp), and a copper box containing a pouch of Holy Salt and a scroll of Transmute Water.

Burial niches line all the walls from floor to ceiling. All are lined with thick layers of cursed ash. Searching the niches thoroughly takes six turns and yields 198gp in coins and a brass trumpet worth 300gp.

Very Dead End

Statue: an imposing statue of a nude skeletally emaciated woman stands against the west wall. In its hands rests The End, clearly not a part of the statue.

Corpses: two corpses lie sprawled on the ground, each with one arm stretched towards the statue. One is centuries old and already looted, the other more recent, carrying a Mace of Gore, plate armour with a helmet in the shape of a lion's head, 35gp in coins, and a gold chalice worth 100gp.

Burial niches line all the walls from floor to ceiling. If disturbed, three Skeletons animate and climb out. One takes The End from the statue and wields it against the tomb robbers. Once the skeletons are dealt with, searching the niches thoroughly takes six turns and yields 110gp in coins and a Comforting Badger.

Hall of Goodbyes

Stone slab: at north end of room, used for processing corpses before burial or cremation. Its surface is pitted with chemical stains.

Pews: line south end of room in two columns.

A trilogy of softcore wererat erotica. Will Lady Lilibeth marry her cleanliness-obsessed suitor, or will she succumb to the forbidden rugged charms of Gregory the sewer man? Readers gain +1 to reaction rolls towards rats, and learn the phrase "I love you" in the Secret Tongue of Vermin.

The Strings That Pull

This old leatherbound volume is divided into two halves. The first comprises twelve scripts of short plays to be performed using puppets, each bloodier and more macabre than the last. A spellcaster closely reading the latter half will gain insights granting a -2 penalty to saves against any of the reader's Hold or Charm spells, but will also gain a compulsion to always prepare at least one Hold or Charm spell no matter the circumstances.

Astral Clay

Value: 50gp per lb

Fighters, knights, and barbarians recognise this rare clay, to others it appears to be mundane dirt. If kneaded with 200gp of powdered meteorite iron per lb, fired into porcelain, and cut cleanly, it produces a supernaturally sharp edge. Weapons made with with astral porcelain inflict double damage, but shatter into useless shards on a to-hit roll of 1. 2lb of astral clay produces enough porcelain to craft a dagger, javelin, spear, or ten arrowheads. 5lb is enough to craft a glaive or shortsword. Larger blades are too difficult for most smiths.

Charnel Lead

Value: 10gp per lb (ore), 100gp per lb (metal)

Clerics and paladins recognise this rare ore, to others it appears to be mundane lead ore. If smelted and quenched in at least ten pints of holy water, it produces a holy metal that makes excellent bludgeoning weapons (+1 to hit and damage) which are especially effective against undead (save vs death or die when struck). 5lb of ore produces enough metal to craft a club, mace, warhammer, or ten sling bullets.

Daisy-of-the-Meadow

Value: 0gp

Meaning: addition, combination

A large lopsided white daisy. It has no effects alone, but can be used to combine the effects of other substances.

Devil's Powderbrush

Value: 50gp

Meaning: earth, stone

Ugly squat purple thistles. They grow in areas of high mineral concentrations. The spines are as sharp as broken glass, and the flowers could make good caltrops or perhaps a single-use dagger.

Dragon Poppy

Value: 100gp

Meaning: fire

Bright orange poppies. The seeds are highly explosive: a single flower could blow open a lock, and a pile of ten is equivalent to a Fire Ball spell.

False Lotus

Value: 5000gp

Meaning: death

Despite the name, it has no resemblance to a lotus, looking more like a pale grey spider lily. It can be used as an extremely effective poison: 1% chance of detection, causes instant death (no save). It is widely considered to be extinct.

Feathergrass

Value: 0gp

Meaning: numbers (e.g. two strands of feathergrass indicates the number two)

Long feathery strands of grass. They have little use besides stuffing mattresses.

Magic Beans

Value: 50gp per bean

Acrobats, rangers, and druids recognise these rare beans, to others they appear to be mundane seeds. If watered every day for three weeks with water purified by Purify Food and Water or similar magic, they will sprout into beanstalks. These beanstalks can be carved into weapons that strike true (+4 to hit but no bonus to damage). One beanstalk can produce the shaft of one staff, spear, polearm, or ten arrow shafts. Beanstalks only produce more magic beans if pollinated by extremely rare ethereal beetles, making cultivation impractical.

Meatbloom

Value: 300gp

Meaning: life, flesh

A foul-smelling large veiny bulbous pink flower. It grows in rotting meat. Ingesting it on its own restores 1d4hp and, if under the effects of a poison or disease, permits a reroll to the save to resist it.

Tenebrous Lichen

Value: 100gp per dose

Thieves and assassins recognise this rare lichen, to others it appears to be mundane bluish-black moss. If mixed with a drop of blood from a human baby it produces an extremely effective paralytic contact poison. If touched with bare skin the victim must save vs death with a -4 penalty or be paralysed for 3d6 turns. It has only a 5% chance of detection. Tenebrous lichen grows on the scale of decades, making cultivation impractical.

Widow's Bellflower

Value: 10gp

Meaning: subtraction, loss

Black droopy foxglove-like bells. It produce copious amounts of soporific pollen: if inhaled, save vs death or fall unconscious for 1d6 turns. If added to a substance, it removes from it the effects of the next substance added.

Winter Lily

Value: 250gp

Meaning: water

A delicate pale blue water lily with black leaves that grows only in pure water. It can be crushed to produce a clear liquid that is an excellent lubricant or solvent.

Wizardstone

Value: 1000gp each

Wizards, illusionists, and bards recognise (and know to look for) these blue-grey gizzard stones that form in the stomachs of magical creatures. The energy within can be expended to recharge a magic item (reroll the initial number of charges and add it to the current number), to cast a single spell as though the caster was five levels higher, or as a substitute for 4000gp and eight weeks towards magical research.
